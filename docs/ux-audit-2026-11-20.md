# UX audit — LegacyMD Mission Control

> **Status, 2026-11-21:** the HIGH findings and U2–U7 are closed; see
> [`../memory/next-actions.md`](../memory/next-actions.md) and the session summary of
> 2026-11-21. Two cautions when reading this report: the base it measured carried
> **test fixtures** (the 3.750,00 return in the expenses finding was deleted the next
> day), and several screens were rebuilt afterwards, so a number here may not
> reproduce. Re-check a MEDIUM/LOW finding before acting on it.

**Date:** 20 November 2026 · **Viewport pass:** 1440×950 (primary) and 390×844 · **Theme:** dark, as served
**Method:** Playwright, all 17 routes plus every dialog I could open, read-only — no form was submitted, so anything that depends on post-submit behaviour is marked *unverified* rather than guessed.
**Evidence:** all screenshot paths below are relative to the repo root (`.tmp/ux-audit/…`).

**State of the data at audit time** (so empty screens are not mistaken for defects): 3 sold lines on 20 Nov 2026, all under a patient literally named `test`; all three fully refunded; no bookings; no open installments; 216 patients with no phone number visible on any row; 7 expense months (May–Nov 2026), November still open.

---

## Verdict in five lines

1. **Not shippable to a non-technical owner today** — the transaction path is good, but the product opens by telling the owner it does not exist.
2. The sale screen is the best-built thing here: patient → line → payment → record, one screen, sensible defaults, one primary button.
3. The first screen reads *"Sold this month — Sales module not built yet"* while the Expenses screen, **one click away**, shows **$22,349.92** sold this month.
4. **The one thing that hurts most:** those four dashes on the dashboard. A front desk that reads "not built yet" over its own live revenue will stop trusting every number in the product, including the correct ones.
5. **Second most:** the owner's stated question — *what did each treatment leave* — has no screen; both "Sales report" and "Reports" are placeholders that say *"the screen is the next slice"*.

## Findings

### CRITICAL

---

**C1 · Dashboard — the four KPI cards and the page title**
**Screen/element:** `/` — "Sold this month", "Collected", "Balance owed", "Show-up rate"; H1 "Mission Control"; "Prototype" badge in the global header.
**What is wrong:** all four cards render an em dash and a sentence denying that the module exists — "Sales module not built yet", "Receipts module not built yet", "Balance view is live, no screen yet", "Appointments module not built yet" — while sales, receipts (returns/payments), balance and appointments are all built and populated. The page title is the internal project name, and every screen carries a "Prototype" badge.
**What a user experiences:** the owner opens the app on Monday morning, sees four dashes and a "Prototype" label, and concludes the software is not ready for her. She goes back to Excel. The real numbers are one click away and she has no reason to look.
**Rule:** *Content · Placeholder Content* — **Do:** "Use realistic sample data"; **Don't:** "Lorem ipsum everywhere" (severity: Low) — and *Feedback · Empty States* — **Do:** "Show helpful message and action"; **Don't:** "Blank empty screens" (severity: Medium). The rule's floor is "show something real"; this screen falls below it by asserting the opposite of the truth.
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/dashboard-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/dashboard-390.png` (contradiction), `.tmp/ux-audit/expenses-1440.png` (the same month, real numbers).
**Fix:** Replace the four cards with the live figures the app already computes — collected this month, balance owed, new vs returning patients, show-up rate — and drop "Mission Control" and the "Prototype" badge from anything the owner sees.

---

**C2 · "Sales report" and "Reports" — two nav destinations that lead to developer notes**
**Screen/element:** `/sales/report` and `/reports`, both reachable from the sidebar.
**What is wrong:** both are placeholders whose body is an internal roadmap: *"The data layer is ready; the screen is the next slice"*, *"The filters are the next decision; the numbers come from the same lines"*, *"This report groups them by the filters we define next"*. "Sales report" is in the **Sales** group (sixth item of six), "Reports" is in **Insights** — two entries promising the same thing.
**What a user experiences:** she looks for "what each treatment left", clicks the most obvious label, and is told by the product that this screen has not been written yet. That is the second of her four questions with no answer, and it is a nav item she will click every week.
**Rule:** *Feedback · Empty States* — **Do:** "Show helpful message and action"; **Don't:** "Blank empty screens" (severity: Medium); *Content · Placeholder Content* — **Don't:** ship placeholder content into a production path (severity: Low).
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/sales-report-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/reports-1440.png`.
**Fix:** Build the margin-by-treatment view into one of them and delete the other from the menu; until it exists, remove both entries rather than promising a screen that is not there.

### HIGH

**H1 · New sale — the primary action sits below the fold, and the total is never beside it**
**Screen/element:** `/sales/new`, the "Record sale" button and the two totals ("Total $0.00" in Lines, "Sale total $0.00" in Payment).
**What is wrong:** with one line added the page is 1100px tall and "Record sale" sits at y=1040 in a 950px viewport; the amount to be recorded appears twice, in two different labels, and neither is near the button. The button is not sticky and there is no summary band.
**What a user experiences:** she fills the form, cannot see the action, scrolls, and then submits without ever re-reading the figure she is committing — the last total she read was "$2,220.00", roughly 350px above the button. With a long sale (several lines) the gap grows.
**Rule:** the knowledge base has no rule for "primary action below the fold"; the closest is *Content · Essential Text Truncation* — **Do:** "Wrap, stack, resize or provide a visible full-detail path"; **Don't:** "Clamp essential meaning only to make cards uniform" (severity: Critical), read as: the action and the amount it commits must be available together, not clipped apart.
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/sales-new-1440.png` (empty), `.tmp/ux-audit/flow-4-line-added.png` (one line, button off-screen), `.tmp/ux-audit/flow-5-before-submit.png` (scrolled to the button).
**Fix:** Pin a slim footer to the sale screen holding the running total and "Record sale", and delete one of the two duplicate totals.

**H2 · New sale — the item picker cannot tell two "Botox" apart**
**Screen/element:** `/sales/new`, "Add from the catalog" results.
**What is wrong:** typing "Botox" returns two items named "Botox Membership" (**$2,220.00**, `MEM-0001 · Membership`) and "Botox Treatment" (**$600.00**, `TRT-0003 · treatment`) — a 3.7× price difference, and the type label is capitalised inconsistently between the two rows. Nothing marks which is the injectable she sold. Arrow keys do nothing in the result list and Enter does not select, so the fast rhythm "type → Enter" fails and the pointer is the only way in.
**What a user experiences:** she picks the first row, records a $2,220 membership for a $600 botox visit, and the month's margin is wrong with no error anywhere. This is the one place in the product where a wrong number gets committed silently.
**Rule:** *Accessibility · Screen Reader* — **Do:** "Use semantic HTML and ARIA properly"; **Don't:** "Div soup with no semantics" (severity: Medium) — the results are a bare list of buttons with no combobox/listbox semantics, so there is no arrow-key model and no announcement that results appeared.
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/flow-3-catalog-search.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/flow-4-line-added.png` (the wrong row, chosen by the first match).
**Fix:** Put the price and the kind at the head of each result row with a clear visual rank, prefer priced and active items, and give the list real combobox semantics so arrows and Enter select.

**H3 · Mobile — the catalog's Name column collapses to zero and the headers collide**
**Screen/element:** `/catalog/treatments` (and the other four tabs) at 390px.
**What is wrong:** the header cell "Name" measures **0px wide** and sits at the same x as "Cost today", so the header row renders as **"Namtost today"** — two labels superimposed. Every treatment name is therefore invisible: rows read `TRT-0001 | $43.00 | $82.00`, code and prices with no idea what is being priced.
**What a user experiences:** she opens the catalog on her phone to check a price before quoting it, and gets a table of codes. The one column that identifies the item is the one that disappeared.
**Rule:** *Accessibility · Text Reflow and Spacing* — **Do:** "Use fluid sizes, content-driven height and unitless line height"; **Don't:** "Clip text in fixed-width or fixed-height boxes" (**severity: Critical**); and *Content · Essential Text Truncation* — **Don't:** "Clamp essential meaning" (**Critical**). "Distinguishing names" is named explicitly in that rule.
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/catalog-390-header.png` (the collision, close up), `.tmp/ux-audit/catalog-treatments-390.png` (full page).
**Fix:** Give the name a minimum share of the row on narrow screens and move the money columns into the scrollable remainder, never the identifier.

**H4 · Mobile — the money columns are off-screen in five tables, with no affordance**
**Screen/element:** `/patients/report` (table 760px in a 356px wrapper), `/sales/details` (574px), `/expenses` (817px), `/open-items` (796px), `/appointments` (944px).
**What is wrong:** each table keeps its full desktop column set and scrolls horizontally inside an unmarked container. On `/sales/details` the amounts are cut mid-column — rows end at "…1 $" — so the screen titled *"what was charged"* does not show what was charged. On `/patients/report` only Name, Phone and Spent are visible; Balance and Status are off-screen.
**What a user experiences:** she sees a clipped table, assumes the app is broken, and does not discover the inner scroll. Page-level horizontal scroll is zero on all 17 routes — so the layout passes the letter of the rule and fails its intent.
**Rule:** *Responsive · Horizontal Scroll* — **Do:** "Ensure content fits viewport width"; **Don't:** "Content wider than viewport" (severity: High); *Responsive · Table Handling* — **Do:** "Use horizontal scroll or card layout"; **Don't:** "Wide tables breaking layout" (severity: Medium).
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/sales-details-390.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/patients-report-390.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/expenses-390.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/appointments-390.png`.
**Fix:** On narrow screens drop to the two or three columns that answer the question (name, amount, state), put the rest behind a row expansion, and show a visible scroll hint where a table genuinely must scroll.

**H5 · Expenses — the month row does not add up, because returns are missing from it**
**Screen/element:** `/expenses`, the November 2026 row; and `/expenses/2026-11-01`, same row.
**What is wrong:** the row shows Sold **$22,349.92**, Total **$29,963.67**, Left **−$11,363.75**. On screen, 22,349.92 − 29,963.67 = −7,613.75. The difference is exactly the **$3,750.00 returned**, which appears only in a KPI card above and is never a column. The header "Sold" in a table titled *Expenses* is itself ambiguous — it is revenue sitting between expenses and profit.
**What a user experiences:** she checks the month with a calculator, gets a different number, and cannot find the $3,750 anywhere in the row. The next step is to stop trusting the screen, then the app.
**Rule:** no rule in the knowledge base covers "a total the reader cannot reproduce"; the nearest is *Content · Number Formatting* — **Do:** "Use thousand separators or abbreviations"; **Don't:** "Long unformatted numbers" (severity: Low) — the numbers are formatted, the row simply omits its own subtrahend. Flagging this as a gap in the rule set rather than borrowing a rule that does not fit.
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/expenses-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/expenses-month-1440.png`.
**Fix:** Add a Returns column (or show net Sold) in the row so the Left figure can be reproduced from the row itself, and rename "Sold" to "Revenue before returns".

**H6 · Sales details — no total, a permanently empty Status column, and rows that go nowhere**
**Screen/element:** `/sales/details`, the table.
**What is wrong:** seven columns, no footer total, and "Status" renders an em dash on every one of the three rows with no legend saying what a status could be. The only clickable thing in a row is the patient name; the line itself cannot be opened, so you cannot see the payment method, the return, or the void attached to what you are looking at. The date is shown three times (toggle, picker, chip beside the count).
**What a user experiences:** she opens the screen to answer "how much came in today", finds three amounts and has to add them herself; the Status column reads as a broken feature; clicking a row does nothing, so she cannot get from a line to the patient's balance without a detour.
**Rule:** *Feedback · Empty States* — **Do:** "Show helpful message and action"; **Don't:** "Blank empty screens" (severity: Medium), applied to a column that is present but always empty. (No rule covers "a row you cannot open"; the closest datapoint is *Data Entry · Bulk Actions* — **Don't:** "Single row actions only" (severity: Low), which argues for more per-row reach, not less.)
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/sales-details-1440.png`.
**Fix:** Add a footer total for the filtered set, either populate Status (paid / partial / refunded / voided) or remove the column, and make the whole row open the sale.

**H7 · Returns, Voids, Open Items — screens with no action, whose empty states point at actions that do not exist**
**Screen/element:** `/sales/returns`, `/sales/voids`, `/open-items`.
**What is wrong:** Returns says *"Nothing to return today"* and *"Search a patient to look further back"*; searching a patient answers *"That patient has nothing to return"* with no control to proceed. **There is no "Record a return" button anywhere on the page.** Voids shows three empty messages in one viewport. Two of the four dialogs named in the brief ("Record a return", "Record payment") could not be opened because the screens expose no trigger with this data — that is the finding, not a limitation of the audit.

**H8 · New sale — leaving the screen discards the whole sale, silently**
**Screen/element:** `/sales/new`; the sidebar, and browser Back.
**What is wrong:** after choosing a patient and adding a line, one click on any sidebar item and Back returns an **empty** screen: patient gone, line gone, payment reset. No warning, no draft, no browser prompt.
**What a user experiences:** at a front desk, someone walks up mid-entry, she clicks Dashboard to answer a question, comes back, and re-enters the sale. This is the most likely data-loss event in the product and it is unguarded.
**Rule:** *Navigation · Back Button* — **Do:** "Preserve navigation history properly"; **Don't:** "Break browser/app back button behavior" (severity: High), extended to the in-progress state the back button is supposed to return to.
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/flow-4-line-added.png` (state before), verified programmatically: after Dashboard → Back, "Returning" chip absent, total back to $0.00.
**Fix:** Keep the in-progress sale (a draft or a confirm-on-leave), and at minimum warn before navigating away with unsaved lines.

**H9 · New sale — the inline "create patient" form has no visible labels**
**Screen/element:** `/sales/new`, "Not in the list? Create the patient" — "Full name" and "Phone".
**What is wrong:** both fields are identified only by placeholder text; neither has a label element, `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` (verified in the DOM: `labels: 0`). Two fields, both empty-looking once typing starts — the phone number is a bare row of digits with nothing saying what it is.

**Rule:** *Content · Input Labels* — **Do:** "Always show label above or beside input"; **Don't:** "Placeholder as only label" (severity: High); *Accessibility · Form Labels* — **Do:** "Use label with for attribute or wrap input"; **Don't:** "Placeholder-only inputs" (severity: High).
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/sales-new-1440.png` (the dashed sub-form), `.tmp/ux-audit/flow-1-patient-search.png`.
**Fix:** Label both fields visibly ("Full name", "Phone"), and state that a phone is what will disambiguate two patients with the same name.

**H10 · New booking — the validation message appears far from the field and is not announced**
**Screen/element:** `/appointments` → "New booking" dialog, "Book" pressed with an empty form.
**What is wrong:** the dialog stays open and prints *"Search the patient, or type a name of at least 2 letters."* in red at the **bottom** of the form, below the time, expect and channel controls — three fields away from the "Search by name" box it refers to. **Fix:** Put the message under the patient search box, mark that field as invalid, announce it as an alert, and focus it.

**H11 · Every screen — no loading feedback on navigation, one error boundary in the whole app, a raw 404**
**Screen/element:** all routes; `/expenses/2099-01-01`.
**What is wrong:** there is no `loading` state for any route — a navigation leaves the previous screen frozen with no progress indicator (verified at 120ms: no loading text, no skeleton). Only `/catalog` has an error boundary; if any other screen throws, the owner gets the framework default. A mistyped month gives the unstyled Next.js **"404 — This page could not be found."** inside the app shell, with no way back to Expenses except the sidebar.
**What a user experiences:** she clicks and nothing happens for a second, so she clicks again; and a typo in a URL produces a screen that looks like the app crashed.
**Rule:** *Feedback · Loading Indicators* — **Do:** "Follow platform and component guidance; preserve layout focus and accessible busy status"; **Don't:** "Apply one timing threshold to every operation or leave long waits unexplained" (severity: High); *Animation · Loading States* — **Do:** "Use skeleton screens or spinners"; **Don't:** "Leave UI frozen with no feedback" (severity: High).
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/nav-pending-120ms.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/bad-month-1440.png`.
**Fix:** Add a shared route-level loading state and a shared error/not-found screen written for the owner ("That month does not exist — back to Expenses").

**M1 · Patient report — two of nine columns are empty on every row I could see, no sorting, and a count that changes meaning**
**Screen/element:** `/patients/report`, the table and the "216 patients" chip.

**M2 · Dates are US-format on a Spanish-speaking owner's screen**
**Screen/element:** `/sales/new` "Business day" (`11/20/2026`), `/patients/new` and the booking dialog (`mm/dd/yyyy`), `/sales/details` (`November 20th, 2026`), `/sales/returns` and `/sales/voids` (`Nov 20, 2026`).
**What is wrong:** four date formats across six screens — `11/20/2026`, `mm/dd/yyyy`, `November 20th, 2026`, `Nov 20, 2026` — and the one the owner types is month-first. For a reader who expects day-first, `11/20/2026` is not a valid date and `03/04/2026` is ambiguous in both directions. "Business day" is also a schema concept (`occurred_on`) leaking into the label where "Date of sale" is what she means.
**What a user experiences:** she hesitates on the one field that decides which day's revenue the sale lands in — and that hesitation is the exact failure the field exists to prevent.
**Rule:** *Content · Date Formatting* — **Do:** "Use relative or locale-aware dates"; **Don't:** "Ambiguous date formats" — example given: "01/02/03" (severity: Low, but the consequence here is a misdated sale).
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/sales-new-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/patients-new-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/sales-details-1440.png`.
**Fix:** Pick one written format across the app ("20 Nov 2026"), and label the sale field "Date of sale".

**M3 · Vocabulary — several labels are the schema, the developer, or the bot talking**
**Screen/element:** appointments ("Expects", "Source", "Via", *"The bot books; you mark the outcome"*, *"The bot's bookings land here"*); patients ("Settled"); returns ("Can return"); open items / expenses / catalog footnotes (*"Recording a payment appends a receipt; cancelling an installment stops expecting it, with a reason. Neither one edits the sale."*, *"The monthly report is the authority for realized margin"*, *"The category here is a suggestion; the sale line records the real one"*).
**What is wrong:** "Expects" is not English, "Via"/"Source" are two columns of provenance with no explanation, "the bot" is an internal actor the owner never chose, and four screens end with a paragraph explaining the storage model. The catalog subtitle openly tells her not to trust the catalog ("the category here is a suggestion").
**What a user experiences:** she reads the last line of a screen and is told the data above it is approximate — so she stops reading the footnotes, and then stops trusting the screens.
**Rule:** *Content · Compact Label Semantics* — **Do:** "Choose static or interactive markup from the label's meaning and ownership"; **Don't:** "Make every pill clickable or encode status with color alone" (severity: High). Stated generally: a label should say what the thing means to her, not how it is stored.
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/appointments-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/open-items-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/catalog-treatments-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/patient-detail-1440.png`.
**Fix:** Rename columns in her words ("Coming for", "Booked by"), replace "the bot" with "online bookings", and move the model explanations into a "?" beside the heading.

**M4 · The same concept gets two names on the same screen**
**Screen/element:** `/sales/new` — "How did the patient pay?" (paid in full) vs "Method" (partial/installments); "Total" vs "Sale total". `/patients/report` — "Patient report" in the menu, "Patient report" as the page title, but "Patients" on the dashboard card.

**M5 · Controls that are the wrong size or give no sign they are controls**
**Screen/element:** `/expenses` month rows — the link is **108×17px** inside a 38px row, the row has no hover state and `cursor: auto`; `/expenses/2026-11-01` — 24×24px pencil and eye icon buttons per row; `/` mobile drawer — every nav row is **32px** tall; `/expenses/2026-11-01` — the category pills ("Payroll & Taxes $14,880.00") are table cells styled like filters.
**What is wrong:** the month row looks like a row and behaves like a caption; the pencils are at the WCAG floor with 15 identical pairs stacked down the page; the mobile drawer's 14 destinations are all under the 44px touch recommendation; the category pills look tappable and are not.
**What a user experiences:** she clicks the word "November" and hits; she clicks "Payroll & Taxes" expecting a filter and nothing happens; on a phone she mis-taps a nav row and lands on the wrong screen.
**Rule:** *Accessibility · Target Size (Minimum)* — **Do:** "Use at least 24 by 24 CSS px"; **Don't:** "tiny adjacent icon buttons" (severity: High); *Responsive · Touch Friendly* — **Do:** "Increase touch targets on mobile"; **Don't:** "Same tiny buttons on mobile" (severity: High); *Interaction · Hover States* — **Do:** "Change cursor and add subtle visual change"; **Don't:** "No hover feedback on clickable elements" (severity: Medium).
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/expenses-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/expenses-month-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/nav-drawer-390.png`.
**Fix:** Make the whole month row a link with hover, raise the drawer rows to touch height, and make the category pills either real filters or plain text.

**M7 · "Close month" is dressed as a primary action, next to "Add expense"**
**Screen/element:** `/expenses/2026-11-01`, header buttons.
**What is wrong:** "Add expense" and "Close month" are two identical solid-indigo primary buttons side by side. Closing a month freezes every edit in it; adding an expense is routine. The confirmation dialog is good — but the button you press to get there is the one that looks safest.
**What a user experiences:** a mis-click lands her in a consequential dialog she did not mean to open. The dialog saves her, so the cost is a scare, not data — but she learns to distrust the header.
**Rule:** *Interaction · Confirmation Dialogs* — **Do:** "Confirm before delete/irreversible actions" (severity: High) — satisfied; the gap is *Interaction · Disabled States*' neighbour, visual weight: **Don't:** "Confuse disabled with normal state" (Medium), read as: a consequential action must not share the accent colour of a routine one.
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/expenses-month-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/dialog-close-month.png`.
**Fix:** Make "Close month" an outline/secondary button and keep the accent for "Add expense".

---

**M8 · The payment method list mixes rails, cards and financing partners, and offers "Unspecified"**
**Screen/element:** `/sales/new`, "How did the patient pay?".
**What is wrong:** one list holds **Credit, Stripe, Cash, Zelle, Cherry, Groupon, Care Credit, Unspecified**. Stripe is a processor, Zelle is a rail, Care Credit and Cherry are financing partners, Groupon is a voucher, "Credit" is ambiguous on its own (card? store credit?) — and "Unspecified" is a legitimate-looking choice that guarantees unusable data later.
**What a user experiences:** she is asked to classify one payment into eight overlapping categories, guesses, and the monthly reconciliation (done by hand against Stripe, per the product's own design) no longer matches.
**Rule:** *Content · Compact Label Semantics* — **Do:** "Choose static or interactive markup from the label's meaning and ownership"; **Don't:** "Make every pill clickable or encode status with color alone" (severity: High) — i.e. keep one dimension in one list.
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/sale-payment-methods.png`.
**Fix:** Split into "how" (Cash / Card / Transfer / Voucher) and "which" (processor or financing partner) only where it matters, and remove "Unspecified".

**M9 · Numbers are formatted two ways on the same row**
**Screen/element:** `/sales/new`, the line editor — "Unit price" input shows `2220`, the line total beside it shows `$2,220.00`; `/patients/report` — page footer "1–20 of 216 · page 1 of 11" wraps to two lines at 390px.
**What is wrong:** an editable amount is a bare integer, the same amount one column over is currency-formatted. On the sale screen the owner reads a number, edits it, and the formatting changes under her.
**What a user experiences:** small, constant doubt about whether the field is dollars or cents, and whether the two figures are the same thing.
**Rule:** *Content · Number Formatting* — **Do:** "Use thousand separators or abbreviations"; **Don't:** "Long unformatted numbers" (severity: Low).
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/flow-4-line-added.png`.
**Fix:** Format the price field as currency on blur and keep the separators while editing.

**M10 · Typography and title case drift between screens**
**Screen/element:** `/patients/[id]` KPI labels are **uppercase** ("SPENT", "RETURNS", "BALANCE"); every other KPI card in the app is sentence case ("Sold this month"). Sidebar mixes "Open Items" with "Sales details", "New sale", "Returns". Table headers mix "Type" / "treatment" / "Membership".
**What is wrong:** the same component family is set three ways, and the item-kind label changes capitalisation between the picker and the table.
**What a user experiences:** the patient page reads as a different product from the rest; the type column looks like free text rather than a fixed set.
**Rule:** *Typography · Font Size Scale* — **Do:** "Use consistent modular scale"; **Don't:** "Random font sizes" (severity: Medium), read across to casing as part of the same consistency contract.
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/patient-detail-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/dashboard-1440.png`, `.tmp/ux-audit/flow-3-catalog-search.png`.
**Fix:** One casing rule for card labels, one for section titles, and one canonical spelling per item kind.

**M11 · Every screen announces its name twice, and the action column has no name**
**Screen/element:** all routes — H1 "Sales details" immediately followed by H2 "Sales details"; the last column header on `/open-items`, `/sales/returns`, `/sales/voids`, `/expenses/2026-11-01` is a lone **"·"**.
**What is wrong:** the top bar's page title is a heading of the same text as the page H1, so a screen reader hears the same name twice before any content. The actions column is headed with a middle dot, so that column is unnamed for everyone including assistive tech.
**What a user experiences:** a slow, repetitive reading experience; and a visually unexplained dot where a header should be.
**Rule:** *Accessibility · Heading Hierarchy* — **Do:** "Use sequential heading levels h1-h6"; **Don't:** "Skip heading levels or misuse for styling" (severity: Medium); *Accessibility · ARIA Labels* — **Do:** "Add aria-label for icon-only buttons" (severity: High) applies in spirit to an unnamed column.
**Evidence:** `.tmp/ux-audit/open-items-1440.png` (the dot), `.tmp/ux-audit/sales-details-1440.png`.
**Fix:** Make the top-bar title a plain non-heading label, and name the last column "Actions" (visually hidden if it must stay clean).

## The sale entry flow, step by step

Measured by doing it, not by reading the screen.

**Returning patient, paid in full, cash** — **6 clicks, 4 decisions, 1 scroll:**

| # | Step | Clicks | Decisions |
|---|------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | Click "New sale" in the sidebar (the dashboard's "Sales" card goes to Sales details, not here) | 1 | — |
| 2 | Click the name field, type ≥2 letters — "Ana" returned **8 patients** | 1 | — |
| 3 | Choose the right one from 8 rows that all read *"no phone · N visits · balance $0.00"* | 1 | **D1 — which patient** (the only disambiguator, the phone, is empty on every patient shown) |
| 4 | Click into the item field and type — "Botox" returned 2 items, $600 and $2,220 | 1 | **D2 — which item** |
| 5 | Click the item; the line appears with Qty 1 and the price prefilled | 1 | **D3 — is the quantity and price right** |
| 6 | Payment: "Paid in full" already selected, "How did the patient pay?" already "Cash" | 0 | **D4 — is it cash** (2 more clicks if not) |
| 7 | Scroll down — the button is at y=1040 in a 950px viewport | — | — |
| 8 | Click "Record sale" | 1 | — |

**New patient** — **7 clicks**, one more than the returning path, and a hidden fifth decision: the inline form captures **name and phone only**. Birth date has no field there, so a complete patient record requires a second visit to `/patients/new` later — a step nothing announces. Creating the patient also cannot be undone from the sale screen if she mistypes.

**Where it stalls:**
1. **Step 3** — the picker cannot distinguish two patients, and arrow keys/Enter do not work, so the pointer is mandatory. 18 Tab presses from the top of the page reach the first field.
2. **Step 5** — two items with the same name and a 3.7× price gap, with the type label capitalised differently between rows. This is where a wrong number gets committed and nothing warns.
3. **Step 7** — the action is off-screen and the total is not with it. The last total she read is $2,220.00, roughly 350px above the button.
4. **Step 8** — *unverified*: I did not submit, so the success state and the post-sale redirect are unknown. There is no loading state on the button to observe beforehand.

**Not counted above, but real:** the "Business day" field must be read and mentally converted (M2), and any accidental click on the sidebar loses everything (H8).

## What is good

- **The transaction screen's shape is right.** One screen, three named sections, patient collapses into a chip that reads *"Returning · 2 visits · balance $0.00 · last Jul 20, 2026"*, "Paid in full" and "Cash" preselected, one primary button. A clerk who has done it once can repeat it.
- **Empty states are written by a human, not a framework.** *"Every sale is collected. When one is sold on a plan, its installments land here."* is better copy than most internal tools ship.
- **Consequential actions are explained before they happen.** "Close the month" states the figure it will freeze and that it can be reopened; "Void the line" keeps the row, requires a reason, and offers "Keep it". The "Void the line" dialog is the best moment in the product.
- **Accessibility fundamentals hold up.** Focus rings are visible on every focusable element I tabbed through (`.tmp/ux-audit/focus-tab3-1440.png`); **zero** icon-only buttons lack an accessible name; every table has a `<th>` header row; muted text measured **6.9:1** and body text **19:1** on the dark theme; the document language is set; dialogs trap focus and close on Escape.
- **No page-level horizontal scroll at 390px on any of the 17 routes** — the layout itself reflows correctly; the failures are inside tables, not in the shell.
- **The catalog is honest about money** — "Cost today" is disambiguated by the day, margin carries its percentage, and the sidebar has one entry for five catalogs rather than five competing ones.
- **Zero console errors** across all 17 routes at both widths.

## Top ten fixes, in the order I would do them

1. **Make the dashboard tell the truth.** Replace the four "not built yet" cards with real figures, and remove "Mission Control" and "Prototype" from the owner's view. *First because it is the only screen that currently argues against the product, and everything else is judged after it.*
2. **Put the total and "Record sale" in a sticky footer.** *Second because it is the daily action; it costs nothing and removes the step where she commits an amount she has not re-read.*
3. **Fix the item picker's ambiguity.** Kind and price at the head of every row, priced/active items ranked first, arrows and Enter working. *Third because this is where a wrong number enters the ledger — the only irreversible mistake a clerk can make in one click.*
4. **Ship one report or delete both entries.** One margin-by-treatment screen, or no "Sales report" and no "Reports" in the menu. *Fourth because the owner's second question has a nav item and no answer, which is worse than no nav item.*
5. **Reconcile the expenses row.** Show returns in the row, or show net revenue. *Fifth because a number she cannot reproduce with a calculator costs more trust than a missing feature.*
6. **Give Sales details a total, a real Status, and an openable row.** *Sixth because "how much came in today" is answered there, and today it needs mental arithmetic.*
7. **Fix the narrow-screen tables: name column never collapses, money columns visible, in-table empty states outside the scroll box.** *Seventh because it is the second device, not the first — but the catalog defect is a hard visual failure, so it must not slide further.*
8. **Give Returns, Voids and Open Items a visible action, and stop the empty states pointing at dead ends.** *Eighth because these are weekly, not daily, and each needs a small dialog rather than a rebuild.*
9. **Lose nothing.** Warn before leaving an unsaved sale, label the two inline patient fields, and stop discarding the draft. *Ninth because the loss is rare per day but total when it happens, and the labels are a one-line fix.*
10. **Close the feedback loop.** A shared route loading state, an error and not-found screen in the owner's words, and field-level validation with an alert. *Last because it is invisible on a fast PC — and the reason to do it last is that until 1–3 are done, no one will be looking at the edges.*
